I. PLEADING STANDARD

To survive a motion to dismiss, a complaint must state a claim that is
plausible on its face. Bell Atl. Corp. v. Twombly, 550 US 544, 570 (2007).
The court accepts well-pleaded facts as true. Ashcroft v. Iqbal, 556 U. S.
662, 678 (2009). Id. at 679.

II. THE CIRCUIT HAS ADDRESSED THIS

The Ninth Circuit reached the same conclusion. Smith v. Jones, 123 F. 3d 456
(9th Cir. 1997). A district court in New York agreed. Alpha Corp. v. Beta
LLC, 700 F. Supp. 1200 (Southern District of New York 1990).

III. THE ANALOGOUS AUTHORITY

Older authority points the same way. Baz v. Qux, 12 Fed. Rep. 34 (1882).
Compare Delta v. Epsilon, 999 F.3d 1 (2d Cir. 1950), which the panel
distinguished. See also Gamma v. Theta, 12 Cal. Rprt. 3d 45 (Cal. Ct. App.
2004).

IV. REMAINING SUPPORT

Plaintiff's reliance on Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113, 99 (1973), is misplaced.
Wrong v. Court, 200 U.S. 1 (9th Cir. 1906), is not to the contrary. Nor is
Future v. Thing, 5 F.4th 9 (1st Cir. 2099).

Finally, as this Court explained in Ghost Corp., supra, at 3, the standard is
settled.
